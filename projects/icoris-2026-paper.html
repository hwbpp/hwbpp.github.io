ICORIS 2026 research paper

Quantifying Data Quality Sensitivity in Tabular Machine Learning Pipelines

A benchmark of how dirty tabular data affects machine learning models: not only accuracy, but also how trustworthy their probabilities and feature rankings stay. It proposes a single robustness score, AUDC.

Status
In the publication process
Venue
ICORIS 2026
Authorship
First author, two co-authors
Scope
4 models · 5 corruption types · 5 severity levels · 2 datasets
icoris-2026-paper.pdf · page 1

01Problem

Real-world tabular data is rarely clean: missing values, noise, outliers, mislabeled rows, and duplicates are common. Most robustness studies only report accuracy.

Decisions such as fraud detection or diagnosis also need calibrated probabilities (a model that says 80% should be right about 80% of the time) and stable feature importance for audits. There was no single benchmark value for how corruption affects all three.

02Data / Context

Two public datasets from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, chosen from different domains so the patterns are not specific to one problem:

  • Adult Income (census): predict whether a person earns more than $50,000 a year. Mixed continuous and discrete attributes, with an imbalanced target. DOI 10.24432/C5GP7S (opens in a new tab)
  • Breast Cancer Wisconsin (Diagnostic): classify tumours as malignant from features computed on images of cell nuclei. All continuous, small, and fairly clean. DOI 10.24432/C5DW2B (opens in a new tab)

03Approach

  1. Corrupt5 types at 0–30% severity
  2. Train4 classifiers, 3 scenarios
  3. Measure5 metrics vs. clean baseline
  4. SummarizeOne AUDC score
  5. TestFriedman + Wilcoxon
  • Corruption: missing values, Gaussian noise, outliers, label noise, and duplicate rows, each at 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, and 30%.
  • Scenarios: corruption in training data only, test data only, or both. Applied after an 80:20 stratified split, with preprocessing fitted on training data only to avoid leakage.
  • Metrics: Accuracy and AUC (prediction), Brier score and Expected Calibration Error (calibration), and Spearman correlation between clean and corrupted feature-importance rankings (stability).
  • AUDC: the relative degradation from the clean baseline at each severity level, integrated over the whole range and normalized. One number per setting; lower means more robust.
  • Statistics: five random seeds; Friedman tests across 30 matched blocks per metric, Wilcoxon signed-rank post-hoc tests with Holm correction, and Kendall's W as effect size.

04Tools

Models
Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, Multi-layer Perceptron
Metrics
Accuracy, AUC, Brier score, ECE, Spearman rank correlation, AUDC (proposed)
Statistics
Friedman test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Holm correction, Kendall's W

05Result

  • Label noise did the most damage (average AUDC 1.0741), followed by outliers (0.9085). Gaussian noise and duplicates had very small effects at the tested levels.
  • Random Forest was the most robust (0.2132); Logistic Regression was the most sensitive (0.6668).
  • Accuracy alone hides the damage: calibration error and feature-importance stability often degraded more than accuracy. Model differences were statistically significant for AUC, ECE, and feature-importance stability, but not for accuracy or Brier score.

Limitations noted in the paper: two benchmark datasets, synthetic random corruption, and binary classification only. The Random Forest result is not a general recommendation.

06My Role

First author. Designed the research framework, implemented the data corruption pipeline, ran all experiments, and wrote the manuscript.

The two co-authors, lecturers from the research course, contributed to the research topic, methodology design, and implementation guidance, and provided review and academic supervision.
